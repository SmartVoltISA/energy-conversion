# E-ENERGY-0012 — Binding / Destruction Symmetry

**Date:** 2026-08-13  
**Status:** COMPLETED / FALSIFICATION PRESSURE  
**Parent hypothesis:** H-ENERGY-04 — structural potential

## Question

Does creating a relation and destroying a relation produce a consistent energetic-like asymmetry in the minimal relational model?

## Model

Four labeled nodes and a common local relation graph are used. Initial state: `0—1     2—3`. Binding adds a cross-relation `1—2`. The structural observable is the same transition-capacity score used previously. No energy variable is introduced.

## Result

For the disconnected two-pair configuration the number of single-edge toggles is 6. For the bound three-edge configuration it is also 6 when disconnected intermediate states are allowed.

Thus the raw toggle-count observable does not distinguish binding from destruction. If connectivity must be preserved, removing the bridge becomes constrained, but the apparent asymmetry comes from the imposed connectivity constraint rather than an independently emerging energy quantity.

The binding operation is directly reversible as a graph edit when disconnected states are permitted. No intrinsic directionality appears.

## Falsification result

The stronger claim that the number of accessible relational changes alone determines an energy-like cost of binding or release does not survive the symmetry test.

## Interpretation

The research must separate:

```text
STRUCTURE → AVAILABLE TRANSITIONS
TRANSITION → DIRECTION / COST
COST / RELEASE → CONSERVATION / TRANSFER
```

Structure alone is insufficient to derive energetic directionality in this model class.

## New hypothesis

**H-ENERGY-05 — Structure + transition rule**: an energy-like quantity may require structure plus a non-symmetric transition rule or an additional state variable that distinguishes accessible change from the cost of change.

## Conclusion

**NEGATIVE for the simplest model; PRODUCTIVE for the research direction.** Structure can constrain possible change, but structure alone does not determine energetic cost or release.
