# E-ENERGY-0010 — Structural Potential

## Question

Can a static relational structure possess a measurable difference in its capacity for future transformation, before any transformation occurs?

This is the first direct test of the new accumulator intuition. The experiment does not assume that this capacity is physical energy.

## Minimal model

Five identical nodes are used. Each structure contains four undirected relations. Three structurally different trees are compared: PATH, STAR, and BRANCHED TREE. No variable named energy is present in the primitive state.

## Local transformation rule

One elementary transformation removes one existing relation and adds one absent relation. The transformation is rejected if the resulting graph becomes disconnected or if any node exceeds degree 3. This is an artificial laboratory control, not a claim about physical dynamics.

## Candidate observable

`P(S) = number of legal one-step transformations available from structure S.`

This measures immediate transformation capacity and is not called energy.

## Result

| Structure | P(S) |
|---|---:|
| Path | 17 |
| Star | 12 |
| Branched tree | 14 |

All three systems have 5 identical nodes, 4 relations, no motion, and no primitive energy variable, yet the number of permitted future transformations differs.

After one legal transformation, the observed P ranges were Path 14–17, Star 14, Branched tree 14–17.

## Observation

A static relational configuration can carry a measurable difference in its capacity for future change. The quantity is determined by structure and allowed transformation rules, not by current motion.

## What the experiment does NOT demonstrate

It does not demonstrate that P(S) is physical energy. P(S) depends on the chosen rewrite rules. It does not establish conservation, quantitative correspondence to work, or necessity of physical space.

Therefore:

```text
structural potential ≠ demonstrated physical energy
P(S) ≠ automatically energy
```

## Hypothesis refinement

Original: Energy is a consequence of a change in relation in space.

Refined: A relational structure can possess a measurable potential for change before the change occurs.

This remains OPEN.

## Next decisive tests

Test rule-independence, additivity, conserved redistribution, reversible versus irreversible transformations, and only then comparison with classical potential energy.

## Interpretation

The strongest outcome is not "we found energy". The idea of a stored structural potential is computationally coherent: a static structure can differ in its capacity for future change.
