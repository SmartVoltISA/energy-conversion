# OMEGA-ENERGY-RELATIONAL-STANDARD v1.0

## Purpose

This document records the Ω-Lab relational treatment of energy as an experimental research framework. It is a working standard, not a replacement for established physical definitions.

## Core separation

```text
SYSTEM STATE
    ↓
INTERACTION
    ↓
TRANSITION
    ↓
ENERGY TRANSFER
    ↓
NEW STATE
```

A relational description may be useful for describing state, accessible transitions, barriers, memory and transfer. It must not silently identify those abstractions with physical energy.

## Mandatory distinctions

- UNKNOWN ≠ TRUE
- HYPOTHESIS ≠ RESULT
- CALCULATION ≠ EXPERIMENT
- STRUCTURAL POTENTIAL ≠ PHYSICAL ENERGY
- TRANSITION BARRIER ≠ ENERGY
- LOCAL RELEASE ≠ CREATION OF ENERGY
- SOURCE ≠ CONVERSION MECHANISM

## Accounting

Every physical conversion hypothesis must eventually define a system boundary and account for:

```text
E_in - E_out = ΔE_system + E_rejected
```

The accounting must include the relevant reservoir and rejected/dissipated channels. An apparent local gain is not evidence of energy creation if another degree of freedom is omitted.

## Conversion graph

```text
SOURCE → CARRIER → INTERACTION → CONVERTER → OUTPUT
```

The central engineering question is not merely where a source exists, but which gradient/potential drives a defined interaction and how much of it reaches useful output.

## Relational findings carried forward

The Ω-Lab experiments show that relational structure can constrain possible transformations, that transition barriers can exist independently of conservation, that history can become part of effective state, and that reversible structure alone does not generate physical energetic directionality.

## Research boundary

The framework becomes physically meaningful only when its abstract states and transitions are mapped to measurable quantities such as field energy, mechanical work, heat, momentum, chemical free energy, electrical energy, or other established physical observables.

## Current continuation

The `energy-conversion` repository continues this work with the stricter chain:

```text
potential → flow → conversion → useful work → rejected energy → verification
```
